Online & Offline Coding

Dealer-level software synchronization, variant SCN coding, module adaptation, parameter calibration, and component protection removal for complex vehicle networks.

Why New & Used Modules Must Be Coded

On modern vehicles, replacing a control unit—such as an ABS/ESP pump, electric power steering rack (EPS), headlight control unit, body control module (BCM), or radar sensor—is never merely a mechanical bolt-on procedure.

Every replacement module arrives either unconfigured (blank) or programmed to a donor vehicle's specific equipment level and VIN. Until it is authenticated and coded to your vehicle’s gateway, it will report "Control Module Incorrectly Coded" and disable warning lights and safety features.

Online vs. Offline Coding Explained

Online Coding: Communicates with manufacturer cloud servers to download the official factory build sheet for your specific chassis number. This matches cryptographic keys and unlocks component protection on used donor parts.
Offline Coding: Involves engineer-level parameter modification for custom feature activation (such as disabling annoying stop-start default states, mirror folding upon locking, acoustic alarm confirmation, or digital speedometer layout modification).
