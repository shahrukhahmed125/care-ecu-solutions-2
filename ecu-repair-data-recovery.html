ECU Repair & Data Recovery

Component-level diagnosis, water damage PCB repair, blown driver IC replacement, and complete 1:1 hardware cloning for engine and transmission control units.

When Engine Computers Fail

The Engine Control Unit (ECU / ECM / DME / DDE) orchestrates all combustion parameters: fuel rail pressure, piezo injector firing pulses, turbocharger wastegates, and throttle body position.

Common catastrophic failures occur when windscreen scuttle drains become clogged with leaves, causing rainwater to submerge the ECU wiring connectors. Other failures arise from jump-start battery reverse polarity, alternator overvoltage spikes, or wiring harness shorts that burn out the internal mosfet driver transistors.

Physical Repair vs. 1:1 Hardware Cloning

Physical Circuit Repair: Under laboratory stereomicroscopes, we clean corrosion via ultrasonic bath, reconstruct burned multi-layer copper PCB traces, and solder replacement power supply regulators, CAN transceivers, and injector driver chips.

1:1 Module Cloning: When an original board is structurally cracked or burned beyond safe physical repair, we connect directly to the microprocessor (Infineon TriCore TC1796/TC1797/TC297, Renesas, or Motorola MPC5xx) via BDM, JTAG, or boot pins. We extract a complete raw binary clone (Flash, EEPROM, and internal CPU memory) and write it into a verified donor unit.

Because the clone is a byte-for-byte exact mirror, the replacement ECU plugs directly back into your vehicle and starts immediately—preserving your original immobiliser rolling codes, keys, mileage, and coding with zero dealer intervention required.

Common ECU Hardware Faults We Resolve

No communication with ECU / "U0100 Lost Communication with ECM"
Single cylinder misfire caused by failed internal injector mosfet
Water ingress, green copper oxide corrosion & broken pin terminals
Brick / failed flash recovery following interrupted OBD remap write
5V sensor reference voltage circuit internal grounding faults
Electronic throttle body or EGR servo drive stage failures
